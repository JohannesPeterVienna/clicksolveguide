Why “where” comes before “how”

A connection to a website passes through five places: your device, its Wi-Fi link, your router, your internet provider and the service itself. A fault in any of them can look the same on screen – a spinning wheel or “No internet”. Generic fix lists don't know which one you have, so they suggest resetting things that were never broken: network settings, browser profiles, sometimes the router itself, with all its settings.

The quickest way to the real cause is to rule out layers with checks that change nothing. Does another device on the same Wi-Fi work? Then the router and the provider are fine, and you can stop looking there. Does the site open on mobile data? Then the website is up. Two or three answers like these usually leave one layer – and the fixes for that layer are short.
