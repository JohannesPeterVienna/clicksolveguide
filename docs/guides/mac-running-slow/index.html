Mac running slow: check these five things first

A slow Mac rarely needs a “cleaner” app. Most of the time one of five things is behind it, and macOS shows you which – in Activity Monitor and in the Storage settings. Check them in this order.

Reviewed November 2, 2026Applies to: macOS 13 Ventura and later; macOS 12 and earlier (paths noted)Sources: Apple Support – Mac User Guide, Activity Monitor User GuidePublisher: AdStern, Vienna

Before you change anything

“Slow” is vague, and the fix depends on what kind of slow you have. A Mac that crawls for a day after a major macOS update is doing scheduled background work and will recover by itself. A Mac that has slowed down gradually over months is more often short on storage or running too many things at login. A Mac that suddenly becomes hot and loud usually has one app or process working flat out.

The five checks below are ordered so that the ones that change nothing come first. Two built-in tools do most of the work: Activity Monitor, which shows what is using the processor, memory and disk right now, and the Storage settings, which show what fills the drive. Neither needs anything installed.

Pick the system you are fixing.

Open Activity Monitor from macOS 13 Ventura or laterFinder > Applications > Utilities > Activity Monitor, or with Spotlight (Command + Space, type “Activity”).

1 · Just updated? Give it time

01

Look for indexing and analysis after an update

Harmlessa few minutes to check

After a major update, macOS rebuilds its Spotlight search index and the Photos app may analyze your library again. In Activity Monitor’s CPU tab you may see processes such as mds, mds_stores or photoanalysisd near the top. This is expected work. Keep the Mac plugged in and awake for a while; it usually settles within hours to a day or so, depending on how many files and photos you have.

2 · Storage nearly full

02

Check how much space is free

Harmlessabout 5 min

Open macOS 13 Ventura or laterApple menu > System Settings > General > Storage. macOS needs free space for memory swap, caches and updates. When the drive is almost full, everything slows down and updates fail. The Storage page lists categories and recommendations, such as emptying the Trash automatically or storing files in iCloud.

03

Remove or move large files

Changes a setting15 min or more

Sort documents by size and look at old downloads, video files and unused apps. Move what you want to keep to an external drive. Delete only what you recognise.

Undo: Deleted files stay in the Trash until you empty it – restore them from there with Put Back.

3 · One app working too hard

04

Sort Activity Monitor by % CPU

Harmlessabout 2 min

In the CPU tab, click the “% CPU” column so the busiest processes are at the top. A browser tab, a sync app or a game stuck at high values for minutes is the likely cause of heat, fan noise and sluggishness. In browsers, each tab may appear as a separate process; the browser’s own task manager shows which site it is.

05

Quit the app properly – or force quit if it hangs

Changes a settingabout 1 min

Quit the app normally first (Command + Q) so it can save. If it doesn’t respond, use Apple menu > Force Quit. Don’t force quit processes you don’t recognise in Activity Monitor; many belong to macOS itself.

Undo: Open the app again. Unsaved changes in a force-quit app may be lost.

4 · Memory pressure

06

Read the memory pressure graph

Harmlessabout 2 min

The Memory tab in Activity Monitor has a small graph at the bottom. Green means memory is fine. Yellow means macOS is compressing and swapping more; red means it is short of memory and the Mac will feel slow. The number of gigabytes “used” matters less than this color – macOS deliberately uses spare memory for caching.

If the graph is often yellow or red, close apps you aren’t using and reduce open browser tabs. If it stays red with only your usual apps open, the Mac may simply have too little memory for your workload.

5 · Too many login items

07

Review what starts with the Mac

Changes a settingabout 5 min

Open macOS 13 Ventura or laterSystem Settings > General > Login Items (called Login Items & Extensions in newer versions). Remove apps you don’t need at startup with the minus button. Below, newer macOS versions list background items you can switch off.

Undo: Add the app again with the plus button, or switch its background item back on.

Skip the cleaner apps.

“Mac cleaner” and “optimizer” apps promise speed but mostly delete caches that macOS rebuilds anyway – and some are adware. macOS’s own Storage settings and Activity Monitor cover everything on this page.

If the Mac is still slow after all five checks, restart it, install pending updates in macOS 13 Ventura or laterSystem Settings > General > Software Update, and contact Apple Support through its official website if problems remain.

Scam protection: what this site never does

ClickSolve Guide never shows a phone number, never offers remote access and never recommends “PC optimizer”, “driver updater” or “cleaner” tools. If a pop-up says your device is infected and gives a number to call, it is a scam: close the browser tab (or the whole browser), don’t call, and don’t let anyone connect to your computer. Real error messages from Microsoft, Apple and Google do not ask you to phone anyone. How to spot fake support pop-ups.

Questions people ask

Why is my Mac slow after a macOS update?

macOS rebuilds the Spotlight index and other background data after big updates, and Photos may reanalyze your library. Keep the Mac on power; it typically recovers by itself within hours to a day.

How much free space does a Mac need?

Apple doesn’t publish one fixed number for everyday use. In practice, keep a comfortable margin free, and more before a major macOS update, which needs several gigabytes to install.

Do I need a Mac cleaner app?

No. macOS manages caches itself, and Storage settings plus Activity Monitor show what you need. Some cleaner apps are classified as adware.

What is kernel_task using so much CPU?

On many Macs, kernel_task uses CPU time to keep the processor cooler when the Mac is hot. Make sure the vents aren’t blocked and the Mac isn’t lying on a soft surface.
