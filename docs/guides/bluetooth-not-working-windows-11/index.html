Bluetooth not working on Windows 11

Bluetooth problems on Windows 11 come in three flavors: the Bluetooth switch is missing, a device won’t pair, or a paired device keeps disconnecting. Each has a different starting point, but all follow the same idea – check the layers from the bottom up.

Think in layers, not in tips

A Bluetooth connection depends on five things stacked on top of each other: the Bluetooth switch in Windows, the Bluetooth adapter in the PC with its driver, a Windows service that manages connections, the pairing record between the PC and the device, and the device itself – its battery and its pairing mode. Fixing a higher layer is pointless while a lower one is broken; a driver reinstall won’t help headphones that are still connected to your phone.

Start at the bottom. Most problems turn out to be layer 1 or layer 5, and both take a minute to check.

Pick the system you are fixing.

Checks that change nothing

01

Check the Bluetooth switch and Airplane mode

Harmlessabout 1 min

Open Windows 11Quick settings (network, sound or battery icon on the taskbar) > Bluetooth tile and make sure Bluetooth is on and Airplane mode is off. The same switch is in Windows 11Settings > Bluetooth & devices.

02

Prepare the other device

Harmlessabout 2 min

Charge it. Put it in pairing mode – often by holding its power or Bluetooth button until a light flashes; the manual says how. Disconnect it from your phone or another computer first: many headphones connect to the last device they knew and ignore new ones.

03

Restart the PC

Harmlessabout 3 min

Use Restart from the Start menu, not Shut down. On many PCs, Shut down with Fast Startup doesn’t fully reset hardware, while Restart does.

Fixing the pairing

04

Remove the device and pair it again

Changes a settingabout 5 min

In Windows 11Settings > Bluetooth & devices > Devices, select the device and choose Remove device. Put the device in pairing mode and add it again with Windows 11Settings > Bluetooth & devices > Add device > Bluetooth.

Undo: Pair it again with Add device; nothing else changes.

05

Choose the right audio output

Changes a settingabout 1 min

Headphones that connect but stay silent are often paired fine – Windows is simply sending sound elsewhere. Select them in Windows 11Quick settings > arrow next to the volume slider.

Undo: Select your previous output device.

When the Bluetooth switch is missing

06

Check the adapter in Device Manager

Changes a settingabout 3 min

Right-click Start > Device Manager and expand Bluetooth. An adapter with a down-arrow is disabled – right-click > Enable device. A yellow warning sign means a driver problem. If there is no Bluetooth category at all, Windows doesn’t see the adapter; check whether your PC has Bluetooth (some desktops don’t) and whether it is switched off in the PC’s firmware settings.

Undo: Disable the adapter again with the same right-click menu if needed.

07

Check the Bluetooth Support Service

Admin neededabout 3 min

Search for “Services”, open it, find Bluetooth Support Service and check that it is running. If it is stopped, right-click > Start. Don’t change other services you don’t know.

Undo: Stop the service again with right-click > Stop (normally not needed).

08

Install the driver from the PC maker or Windows Update

Admin neededabout 15 min

Look in Windows 11Settings > Windows Update > Advanced options > Optional updates, or download the Bluetooth driver for your exact model from the PC or adapter maker’s official support site. Avoid driver updater tools.

Undo: Device Manager > Bluetooth adapter > Properties > Driver > Roll Back Driver (when available).

When a device keeps disconnecting

Bluetooth is a short-range radio that shares the 2.4 GHz band with many Wi-Fi networks. Distance, a body or a metal desk between PC and device, and USB 3 ports or hubs very close to a small USB Bluetooth dongle can all cause dropouts. Move closer, keep a USB dongle away from other plugs (a short USB extension cable helps), and keep the device charged – many devices reduce their radio power when the battery is low.

Windows can also switch off devices to save power. In Device Manager, the adapter’s Properties may have a Power Management tab with “Allow the computer to turn off this device to save power”; unticking it is a reversible setting worth testing on laptops.

No Bluetooth “fixer” downloads.

Everything on this page uses tools built into Windows or drivers from the PC maker. Download sites offering Bluetooth repair tools are unnecessary, and some bundle adware.

Scam protection: what this site never does

ClickSolve Guide never shows a phone number, never offers remote access and never recommends “PC optimizer”, “driver updater” or “cleaner” tools. If a pop-up says your device is infected and gives a number to call, it is a scam: close the browser tab (or the whole browser), don’t call, and don’t let anyone connect to your computer. Real error messages from Microsoft, Apple and Google do not ask you to phone anyone. How to spot fake support pop-ups.

Questions people ask

Why did the Bluetooth toggle disappear in Windows 11?

Windows shows the toggle only when it sees a working Bluetooth adapter. A disabled adapter, a driver problem or the adapter switched off in firmware makes it disappear. Device Manager shows which.

My headphones are connected but there’s no sound. Why?

Windows is probably sending audio to another output. Choose the headphones in the output list next to the volume slider. If they offer two entries (for example “Headphones” and “Headset”), try the other.

Should I uninstall the Bluetooth driver?

Only as a later step. Remove and re-pair the device first, then install a driver from Windows Update or the PC maker. Uninstalling without having the right driver at hand can leave you without Bluetooth.

Why does my Bluetooth mouse lag?

Low battery, distance, interference from Wi-Fi or USB 3 devices, and power-saving settings. Charge it, move the receiver or PC closer, and test without power saving.

Can I fix Bluetooth with a network reset?

Windows’ network reset targets network adapters and settings. For Bluetooth, removing and re-pairing devices and checking the adapter and driver are the relevant steps.
