Reference · routers, firewalls, printers, mail apps

Port numbers you meet while troubleshooting

Router pages, firewall prompts, printer setups and mail apps talk in port numbers: 53, 443, 631, 9100. The official registry has thousands of entries; this page is a selection of the ones behind everyday faults – DNS, address assignment, printing, mail, VPN, device discovery – each with its registered name and the place where it turns up in a diagnosis.

How ports fit the layer map

An IP address finds the device; the port number finds the service on it. A printer can be switched on, joined to the Wi-Fi and reachable by ping and still not print, because the one port the computer uses for printing is not answering or is filtered between two networks. That is why “the printer is on the guest Wi-Fi” and “the PC is on a VPN” are questions in the printer diagnoses.

Service names, transport protocols and descriptions are the registry’s. The column “Where you meet it” is this site’s note. The selection is ours: it covers what the diagnoses and guides on this site touch, not every port in use.

Sources and how this page was made

Port numbers, service names, transport protocols, the registry’s short descriptions and RFC references were taken from the registry’s CSV file; only TCP and UDP rows were used, and alias names for the same port were left out. The selection and the column “Where you meet it” are ClickSolve Guide’s own wording.
